Civil time

enum time_error

#enum time_error

Why timestamp::parse or date::parse refused its text.

Variants

#malformed( u64 index )

The byte at index does not fit; index is the text's size when the text ends early.

#no_such_time

The fields name no such day or time, such as a 13th month or a 25th hour.

#out_of_range

The time lies outside what the type holds: for a timestamp 1677-09-21 to 2262-04-11, for a date the years 1 to 9999.

class timestamp

#class timestamp implements equatable, comparable, hashable

A point in time as nanoseconds since 1970-01-01 UTC, from 1677-09-21 to 2262-04-11, the range of an i64. Leap seconds are not counted, as POSIX does not, and a time read without a zone is taken as UTC.

The time as pandas writes a Timestamp: 2026-11-19 14:03:05, with 6 fraction digits when the fraction is whole microseconds and 9 when it is not.

#public string format( const ref textual pattern ) const

The time written by pattern, in UTC: each directive is replaced by its field and every other byte copied. %Y the year in 4 digits, %m the month and %d the day in 2, %H %M %S the hour, minute and second in 2, %f the microseconds in 6, %j the day of the year in 3, %b and %a the month's and the weekday's English name in 3 letters, %z the offset, always +0000, and %% a %.

Panics

On any other directive.

#public static result<timestamp, time_error> parse( const ref textual text )

The time ISO 8601 writes in text: 2026-11-19, then optionally a space or T and 14:03, 14:03:05 or 14:03:05.123456789 with 1 to 9 fraction digits, then optionally Z or an offset +01:00, +0100 or +01, which the answer has been moved to UTC by.

Errors

malformed at the first byte that does not fit, no_such_time for fields naming no day or time, and out_of_range outside a timestamp's range.

#public static result<timestamp, time_error> parse( const ref textual text, const ref textual pattern )

The time text gives as pattern reads it. A directive reads its field, as format writes it, and every other byte must match itself: %Y 4 digits, %m %d %H %M %S 1 or 2, %j 1 to 3, %f 1 to 9 fraction digits, %b an English month name in 3 letters of any case, %a a weekday's, which is checked against nothing, and %z as parse's offset. The year is 1900, the month and day 1 and the time midnight when the pattern does not give them.

Errors

As parse's.

Panics

On a directive not listed.
